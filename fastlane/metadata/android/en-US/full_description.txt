DAVKeep syncs contacts and calendars from a CalDAV/CardDAV server into Android's
own Contacts and Calendar apps — including servers that a normal client cannot
reach, because they sit behind a gateway that demands more than a password.

Per account, DAVKeep can send:

* arbitrary HTTP headers, for a proxy that wants an authorization header, a
  Cloudflare Access service token, or any other header your gateway requires
* a client certificate (mTLS), chosen from the Android key store, for a server
  that asks for one during the TLS handshake

Everything else is an ordinary DAV client: discovery of the collections an
account can see, two-way sync of contacts and events, per-collection control of
what syncs, and per-collection control of whether edits made on the phone are
sent back to the server. Sending edits is off until you turn it on, one
collection at a time, so a server can never be written to by accident.

What DAVKeep does not do: no ads, no analytics, no crash reporting, no account
of ours, no update check. It talks to the servers you configure and to nothing
else. Credentials and certificates stay on the device.
